Ticket 102 — correct, honestly reported, and the most wasteful run of the three

Rosa pulled lease 1 and confirmed $2,400 due 2026-09-02, not overdue — which matters, because "the landlord is asking" and "we are late" are different tickets. But the ticket turns on two facts — what is owed, and what is in the account — and it cost 13 model calls, 39,020 input tokens and 23.4 seconds. Three things went wrong at once:

  1. The Boss delegated a question and answered it simultaneously. At 1.17s it emitted ask_facilities, review_cash_and_obligations and ask_accounting in a single model response. It asked Dan to check the cash and checked the cash itself, in the same breath. That is not redundancy discovered late; it is redundancy built into one decision.
  2. Customer Service consumed 42% of the wall clock. Theo ran from 8.26s to 18.11s — 9.85 seconds of a 23.4-second ticket — on an agent my plan had explicitly excluded, drafting a note to a landlord about a lease.

Every number Theo needed was in the question. Theo then called review_cash_and_obligations regardless, spending 429 output tokens on it. That is the third call of that same tool on this one ticket.

None of this made the answer worse — all three are read-only tools on mode=ro connections, so the cost is tokens and latency, not correctness. But $2,400 left the account on the strength of two numbers that took 39,020 tokens to confirm.

Two root causes, and the second is more interesting than the first.

I modelled the Boss as a router that owns no tools. It owns all ten. For a one-SKU, one-size question there is nothing Priya can return that check_item_availability("CC-TEE-WHITE","S",501) does not already return — it reports stock, the linked invoice, the vendor and the lead time in one call. The hop I planned had no information to add, so the model skipped it. My plan priced delegation at zero.

I built the plan around a discovery that cannot happen. The chain depended on Inventory noticing that the restock hinges on invoice 501. But get_ticket(101) is the Boss's very first call, and it resolves the linked invoice, vendor and lead time in its return value. The dependency I designed a hand-off around was handed over for free, 1.72 seconds in, before any specialist existed.

102 — right order, wrong shape, one agent too many

I predicted Facilities, then Accounting, as a chain: Rosa verifies the rent and passes the verified figure to Dan. The trail shows the Boss emitting ask_facilities and ask_accounting in a single model response at 1.17s — a fan-out. The consequence is not cosmetic: Dan was given the $2,400 figure by the Boss and began work at 1.18s, while Rosa was still verifying that same figure and did not finish until 1.86s. Accounting validated a number that Facilities had not yet confirmed. It happened to be right. The ordering I predicted would have made that guaranteed rather than lucky.

I also ruled Customer Service out in writing, on the grounds that this arrived as an email from a landlord about a lease, not a customer with a complaint. Theo was called anyway, and cost 9.85 seconds and 8,469 input tokens. I was right about the reasoning and wrong about the behaviour. Inventory I correctly excluded — it never ran.

103 — the plan held exactly

Inventory first, Customer Service for the reply, Accounting only if a price below list is proposed — and because none was, Dan never ran. Facilities stayed out. The conditional clause is the part worth noting: it is the only place in the three plans where I specified a trigger rather than a sequence, and it is the only prediction that survived contact with the run.

The one deviation is an improvement on the plan. The Boss called price_bulk_order before delegating, so the question it sent Priya already contained the shortfall: "we have a 12-unit shortfall for Yale AI Club's request of 20. Is a reprint/restock already ordered…" A sharper question than the one I had drafted, and it is why Priya came back with the absence of a reprint established rather than assumed.

The single sharpest piece of evidence is a tool that never fired

I built consult_teammate(teammate, question) so any specialist could reach any peer, and the 101 plan rested on it entirely. Across all three runs consult_teammate was called zero times. All six delegations came from the Boss: ask_customer_service three times — once on every ticket — and ask_inventory, ask_accounting and ask_facilities once each.

So the team is a hub with five spokes and no rim. Specialists never talk to each other, never see each other's results, and in the one case where two ran at once (102) they worked on overlapping questions in ignorance of each other. Every plan I wrote assumed a network. The system that exists is a star.

What I would predict now: the Boss handles any ticket resolvable from one or two tool calls entirely alone; it delegates only when a specialist's prompt adds judgement the tool output does not — drafting customer prose, or weighing an obligation set; and ask_customer_service fires on essentially every ticket regardless of whether a customer is involved, because every ticket ends in something that reads like a message.

3 · What would have been simpler as one agent with tools

Tickets 101 and 103 should be one agent. Ticket 101 effectively already was. The Boss made four of its five MCP calls and delegated only the writing of a reply; remove the hand-off to Theo and you have a single agent with ten tools producing the identical decision and the identical Cash tab row.

None of it threatens correctness — all four are read-only tools on mode=ro connections, so a stale or repeated read cannot corrupt anything. The cost is tokens, latency and noise in the trail. But "harmless" is not "earning its place".

What the team structure genuinely buys — and it is not better answers

Attribution the approval record needs. "Who did what" on the board, and agent_contributions in output/resolved_tickets.json, only carry meaning because distinct agents did distinct work. A solo agent yields one undifferentiated transcript. When a person signs $2,400 out of a $2,560 account, "Rosa verified the lease, Dan verified the cash against every obligation" is a materially better record than "the agent checked some things" — and ticket 101 is the proof that this record has to be derived from the trail, never self-reported.

Separation of duties at the prompt layer. Accounting's prompt says prepare payments and never authorise them; Inventory's says never promise a date without a lead time. A single agent must hold every restriction simultaneously on every ticket, and the one that slips is the expensive one. Splitting them means the agent reasoning about $2,400 is reading a prompt about money and nothing else.

Independent verification, when it lands on the right number. Theo re-checking price_bulk_order on 103 before quoting $58.00 to Yale AI Club is exactly the duplication you want. The same reflex on 102 was waste. The difference is whether the number is about to leave the building.

The concrete recommendation

  1. Route by ticket, not by default. A stock lookup (101) or a pricing question (103) goes to one agent with the full toolset. A ticket that moves money (102) keeps the split, because two independent named checks are worth 124% overhead when $2,400 is leaving a $2,560 account.
  2. Give delegates the Boss's context. Pass the tool results already fetched into the sub-agent's starting state. That alone removes the 7 redundant get_ticket calls and two of the three cash reads on 102 — most of the waste in this run, without removing a single agent.
  3. Either use consult_teammate or delete it. Zero calls in three runs means it is untested surface area. If peer consultation matters, the specialist prompts have to instruct it explicitly; right now nothing tells Priya that Dan exists.

4 · Three new problems this team could solve with the tools already built

Each of these is triggered by a row sitting in campus_customs_new.db right now, maps onto a ticket the team has already proven it can handle, and needs no tool that does not exist.

A · "Do you have the Crest Mug?" — a second zero-stock SKU nobody has noticed

The trigger. CC-MUG-CREST, Crest Mug, size OS, Aisle D, qty = 0. That is the identical condition that made ticket 101 difficult, on a SKU with no ticket attached and no invoice covering a reprint. The next customer who asks produces ticket 101 again, with less warning and no reprint already in flight.

Why this team can do it. It is 101 with the dependency removed — strictly easier than a ticket already solved correctly.

#AgentCallWhat comes back

B · "Can we make next month's rent?" — asked early instead of on the due date

The trigger. The Cash tab ends at $160.00. leases.monthly_rent is $2,400.00. The shop is $2,240.00 short of a recurring obligation it has just demonstrated it must pay, and nothing on the board says so — because ticket 102 arrived as a notice, and notices arrive late.

Why this team can do it. It is exactly ticket 102, with the trigger changed from an email to a scheduled question. The staffing already worked: Rosa on the lease, Dan on the obligation set.

#AgentCallWhat comes back
1Facilitiesget_lease(1)Chapel Street shop, Elm City Properties, monthly_rent 2400.0, and the payment history now showing the September payment.
2Accountingreview_cash_and_obligations()$160.00 in checking, total_owed 0, covers_everything true.
3Bossclose_ticket(id, "blocked", …)"Next rent is $2,400.00. We hold $160.00. Shortfall $2,240.00." Escalated before it is late.

Dan's behaviour on 102 is what makes this work. He did not check whether this bill could be paid; he checked cash against every obligation. That is the habit that turns a payment question into a solvency question, and it is already in the prompt.

One honest caveat. record_payment settles the rent but does not advance leases.next_due — it still reads 2026-09-02 even though the Cash tab shows it paid. So the team can state the amount and the shortfall correctly today, but it cannot name the next due date without a human or a one-line fix to the write tool. The gap is in the tool, not the agents.

C · Stock that moves without a ticket — including the eight hoodies from ticket 103

The trigger, and it is live right now. Ticket 103 offered Yale AI Club 8 CC-HOOD-NAVY in M. The inventory table still reads 8. Across all three runs adjust_inventory was called zero times. The moment the Club accepts, a damaged-stock count happens, or shrinkage is written off, the book figure and the shelf diverge — and every future check_item_availability answer is wrong, including the ones quoted to customers.

Why this team can do it. The tool is built, guarded and idle:

#AgentCallWhat happens
1Inventorycheck_item_availability("CC-HOOD-NAVY","M")Establishes the book figure: 8.
2Inventoryadjust_inventory("CC-HOOD-NAVY","M",-8,"Sold to Yale AI Club, ticket 103")Writes the new quantity with a reason attached. Refuses anything that would push stock below zero — tested to −1.
3Bossclose_ticket(id, "closed", …)Records the movement and who it was for.

Why it is safe to let an agent write this one unsupervised, when record_payment is not: an inventory correction is reversible by an equal and opposite delta, it cannot overdraw anything, the negative-stock guard makes the worst case a refusal rather than a corruption, and every call carries a mandatory reason into the audit trail. Money is irreversible; a miscount is not. This is the only write tool the team is trusted with, and the run never needed it — it is finished, tested, and waiting.

5 · Three problems this team could not solve, and exactly what each would need

These are not hypothetical. Each one is a thing the run tried to do, or should have done, and could not — and each is blocked by a missing table first and a missing tool second.

A · Actually ordering the reprint that ticket 101 was about

The gap. The Cash tab records $840.00 leaving the account and invoices.status now reads paid. But nothing in this system can place an order. There is no purchase-order table among the nine, and none of the ten tools instructs a vendor to do anything.

Proof that no combination of the ten gets there. Seven tools are read-only. Of the three writes, record_payment touches payments/cash_accounts/invoices, adjust_inventory touches inventory, and close_ticket touches tickets. No tool writes anything a vendor could ever see. Worse, invoices has no quantity column — at an $8.00 unit cost, nobody in the shop can say how many size-S tees $840.00 buys. And Priya's "no reprint is ordered or inbound" on ticket 103 was established by calling list_open_tickets: a proxy for a record that does not exist. Tauhid Zaman's tee has been paid for and cannot be ordered.

The table.

B · Sending the three replies Theo wrote

The gap. Customer Service drafted a reply on all three tickets — 232, 1,052 and 685 output tokens of carefully hedged prose — and none of the three was sent. Proof that no combination of the ten gets there. There is no messages table, so there is nowhere to even store a reply. The inbound email that created ticket 102 has no row in any of the nine tables — its existence survives only as the word "Email:" at the start of tickets.notes.

C · "Can we afford the other twelve hoodies?" — the question Yale AI Club actually asked

The gap. The team answered a narrower question than the one it was asked. It offered 8 and escalated the discount, because it cannot reason about money it does not yet have. review_cash_and_obligations() looks only at money leaving — invoices, leases, payments. In all nine tables there is no sales table, no customer order, and no receivable. The shop can see every dollar it owes and not one dollar it is owed.

Why that is fatal to the answer. "12 × $22.00 = $264.00 against $160.00" reads as impossible. But the 8 hoodies on offer are worth 8 × $58.00 = $464.00, and if that lands before the reprint is due the order is affordable with $360.00 to spare. The honest answer depends entirely on timing, and the system has no concept of time beyond desk.date_today. Separately, nothing in the ten tools can authorise a price below list — price_bulk_order faithfully reports the $22.00 cost and the $58.00 list and then stops, which is why the discount became an escalation rather than a decision.

The additions.

AdditionShapeWhat it answers
customer_orders table id, ticket_id, requester, sku, size, qty, unit_price, placed_at, expected_payment_at, status Makes the 8 hoodies a receivable of $464.00 with a date, instead of a sentence in a draft reply.
pricing_policy table sku, floor_margin_pct, max_discount_pct, approver_required Turns "should we discount?" into a rule. At $22.00 cost and $58.00 list there is 62% of margin; a 10% floor makes a 20-unit discount a decision an agent may take.
forecast_cash(days) Read-only, returns a balance over time Sequences the 2026-09-02 rent against an expected $464.00 and a possible $264.00 reprint, instead of racing them.
quote_discount(sku, qty, proposed_unit_price) Checks against pricing_policy Refuses below the floor; approves within it; escalates between. The _shop_rules.md ban on selling below cost becomes enforceable in code rather than in prose.

The agent. A Planner / Treasurer that reasons across tickets rather than inside one. This is the role the run exposed most clearly, and the Cash tab is the evidence: tickets 101, 102 and 103 were each answered correctly in isolation, review_cash_and_obligations was called four separate times, and not one agent ever observed that funding the first two is what made the third impossible. Every agent on this team is scoped to a ticket. Nobody is scoped to the shop.
